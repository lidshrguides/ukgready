Follow these steps in order. Must be completed within 3 business days of the employee's start date.

The Form

What Is Form I-9?

Form I-9 is the federal Employment Eligibility Verification form. Every employer in the United States is required to complete it for every employee they hire.

🏛️

Federal requirement for every single U.S. hire — no exceptions

🪪

Verifies both the employee's identity and their legal right to work

🔗

The information entered is used to submit the E-Verify case

⚠️
Must be completed within 3 business days of the employee's first day. Section 1 is completed by the employee on or before day one. Section 2 is completed by you within 3 business days.
The Two Sections

I-9 Sections 1 & 2

The I-9 has two sections completed by two different people.

👤 Section 1 — Employee

  • ✓ Completed by the new hire on or before their first day
  • ✓ Personal information (name, address, DOB)
  • ✓ Citizenship or work authorization status
  • ✓ Signature and date
  • — Do not edit Section 1 — it belongs to the employee

👔 Section 2 — Manager

  • ✓ Completed by you within 3 business days of hire
  • ✓ Review acceptable documents in person
  • ✓ Enter document title, issuing authority, number, and expiration date
  • ✓ Sign and date
  • ✓ Verify the information matches Section 1 before submitting
Documents

Acceptable I-9 Documents

Employees must present original, unexpired documents. You cannot tell them which documents to bring — they choose from the lists below.

List A

1 document — establishes BOTH identity and work authorization.

Acceptable alone. No additional document needed.

OR

List B + List C

1 from List B (identity) AND 1 from List C (work authorization).

Both are required together.

Common Document Examples

List A
U.S. Passport Card
Establishes identity + work auth combined. Must be unexpired.
List A
Employment Authorization Document (EAD)
Issued by USCIS. Check expiration date carefully.
List B
State Driver's License
Identity only. Must be paired with a List C document.
List C
Social Security Card
Work auth only. Must be unrestricted. Pair with List B.
List C
U.S. Birth Certificate
Work auth only. Must be a certified copy. Pair with List B.
⚠️
DO NOT tell employees which documents to bring.

This is a federal violation. Employees have the right to present any document from the acceptable lists. You may only tell them that they need to present documents that establish identity and work authorization.

🔗

For the full official list of acceptable documents, refer to the USCIS I-9 Central website at uscis.gov/i-9-central.

Watch Out

Common Issues

These are the most frequent problems encountered during I-9 document review. Know what to look for.

  • ✗

    Expired documents Any document past its expiration date is not acceptable — no exceptions, even if the employee says they have a renewal pending.

  • ✗

    Restricted Social Security Cards Cards stamped "VALID FOR WORK ONLY WITH DHS AUTHORIZATION" or "NOT VALID FOR EMPLOYMENT" are not acceptable as a List C document on their own.

  • ✗

    Information does not match Section 1 The document details you enter in Section 2 must exactly match the information the employee provided in Section 1. If there is a mismatch, correct the error or restart the form before submitting.

E-Verify

What Is E-Verify?

E-Verify is the federal electronic system that cross-references the information on the I-9 against government databases to confirm work eligibility.

🏛️

A federal system operated by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and SSA

🔍

Uses the information from the completed I-9 to verify work eligibility electronically

📋

Required for all U.S. hires — submitted through UKG Ready after Section 2 is complete

⚠️
E-Verify cannot be submitted until I-9 Section 2 is complete. Always finish the I-9 first, then submit E-Verify.
E-Verify Result

Tentative Nonconfirmation (TNC)

A TNC means the information submitted did not match government records. It does NOT mean the employee is unauthorized to work — it means the records need to be reconciled.

⚠️
A TNC is common and does not mean the employee cannot work.

Review the notice with the employee and follow all on-screen prompts in UKG Ready.

What Happens Next

🔔 Employee Chooses to Contest (Take Action)

The employee notifies you that they will contest the TNC, contacts DHS or SSA within the required timeframe to resolve the discrepancy, and the case stays open while they do so. Follow all on-screen instructions in UKG Ready throughout this process.

✓ Employee Chooses Not to Contest

If the employee decides not to contest the TNC, take no further action and close the case in UKG Ready following the on-screen prompts.

✏️ TNC Caused by a Typo or Data Entry Error

This is the most common cause of a TNC. If you entered incorrect information during E-Verify submission, you can edit the information during the review process in UKG Ready before taking further action.

E-Verify Result

Final Nonconfirmation (FNC)

An FNC means E-Verify was unable to confirm work authorization after the TNC contest process concluded.

  • FNC

    Work authorization could not be confirmed. The employee cannot take further action to contest. Follow all instructions displayed on screen in UKG Ready — the system will guide you through the required next steps. Contact hr@lids.com immediately for guidance before taking any employment action.

🚫
Do not take any employment action based on an FNC without first contacting hr@lids.com. There are specific federal requirements for how FNCs must be handled.
E-Verify Result

Case Authorized

This is the result you are aiming for.

  • Case Authorized

    E-Verify has successfully confirmed the employee's work authorization. No further action is needed. Once both the I-9 and E-Verify steps are completed and the case is authorized, the employee's employment eligibility verification process is officially complete.

✅

Once you see Case Authorized, you're done. The employee is cleared to work and their onboarding can continue.
